Today’s strongest signal is convergence: satellite and terrestrial networks are sharing more spectrum, operators are turning advanced radio research into live service, and increasingly capable AI is moving into both cloud infrastructure and resource-constrained orbital systems.

<!-- more -->

## Nokia and ICEYE team up on sovereign satellite communications

Nokia and ICEYE will jointly develop secure, sovereign, high-performance broadband satellite communications systems. The partnership targets defense and border-security missions as well as emergency services and disaster response, where resilient connectivity must bridge space assets and mission-critical terrestrial networks.

Source: [Nokia on X](https://x.com/nokia/status/2105553562285601115)

## FCC opens more than 1,000 MHz for satellite connectivity

At its September meeting, the FCC adopted rules opening spectrum in the 12.7 GHz and 42 GHz bands to more intensive satellite use. The additional capacity is intended to support home broadband, in-flight and maritime connectivity, and core ground-network links—an important policy change as multiple NGSO constellations scale.

Source: [FCC September 2026 Open Meeting](https://www.fcc.gov/September2026)

## e& activates commercial Upper-6GHz Giga-MIMO

e& UAE moved 256TRX Upper-6GHz Giga-MIMO from validation into live 5G-Advanced service. The deployment uses as much as 350 MHz of contiguous spectrum and reportedly reaches peak speeds near 10 Gbps, providing an early commercial example of the wide mid-band channels and advanced beamforming expected to help bridge 5G-Advanced and 6G.

Source: [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/09/30/6g-technology-uae-u6ghz/)

## OpenAI brings agent training to small businesses

OpenAI’s new small-business report describes small teams using AI agents for finding customers, building products, and managing finances. A partnership with America’s Small Business Development Center network will pair the report with hands-on training and local guidance, extending agent adoption beyond large enterprises.

Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/helping-small-businesses-put-ai-to-work/)

## DeepMind watermarks AI-designed proteins

Google DeepMind introduced SynthID Bio, a provenance system that embeds detectable signatures into AI-designed protein sequences while seeking to preserve their biological function. The work applies the watermarking idea to generative biology, where tracing synthetic designs could become an important layer of scientific accountability and biosafety.

Source: [Google DeepMind](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/synthid-bio/)

## CoreWeave brings NVIDIA Vera Rubin into production

CoreWeave is bringing NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems into production as the companies integrate infrastructure for large-scale model training and high-throughput agent inference. The deployment is a useful marker of how quickly next-generation accelerators are moving from announcement to cloud availability.

Source: [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/coreweave-agentic-ai-vera-rubin/)

## Starlink Mobile launches in Ecuador with CNT

Starlink Mobile is now available in Ecuador through state-owned operator CNT. Compatible phones can use messaging and selected apps in cellular dead zones, extending direct-to-device coverage across the country’s coastlines, mountains, and forests without specialized consumer satellite hardware.

Source: [Starlink on X](https://x.com/Starlink/status/2105079009340260529)

## Ericsson reports 5x faster roaming downloads

Real-world tests tied to Ericsson On-Demand Roaming Breakout produced more than fivefold faster downloads and lower latency. The architecture improves roaming performance by breaking traffic out closer to a traveler’s location instead of carrying it through a distant home network—a concrete example of cloud-native mobile-core design improving user experience.

Source: [Ericsson on X](https://x.com/ericsson/status/2105583601358078319)

## Research Radar

### Spectrum Sharing Among Broadband LEO Mega-Constellations

Ian P. Roberts reviews the FCC and ITU rules governing interference among broadband constellations and compares coexistence tools including dynamic satellite selection, passive ground sensing, centralized registries, and bilateral coordination. Particularly relevant are the open problems around scaling beyond two networks, direct-to-cell feeder-link contention, and optical inter-satellite rerouting.

Source: [arXiv:2609.36122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36122)

### SCORAS-MoE: Resource-Adaptive MoE-VLM Deployment in LEO Networks

Tong Quan and collaborators propose jointly compressing and distributing vision-language mixture-of-experts models across satellites. Their scheduler adapts model profiles and shard placement to changing energy and communication conditions, while experiments report better throughput and substantially faster decisions than the evaluated PPO and evolutionary baselines.

Source: [arXiv:2609.36763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36763)

### Interference Analysis Between Fixed Services and 6G Mobile Networks

Samuel S. Silva and collaborators study incumbent fixed links sharing spectrum with future 6G uplinks. Their simulations find coexistence infeasible without mitigation, but show that distance-based protection zones and selective uplink scheduling can satisfy interference limits while preserving useful capacity for admitted users. The paper has been accepted at SBrT 2026.

Source: [arXiv:2609.33860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33860)
