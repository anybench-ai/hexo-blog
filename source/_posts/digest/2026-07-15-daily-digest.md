# Morning Digest for Wednesday, July 15, 2026

## Anthropic launches free Claude for U.S. K-12 teachers
Anthropic is extending premium Claude access to verified K-12 educators in the United States. This matters beyond education: frontier labs are increasingly competing through institutional distribution, trying to become embedded in schools, workplaces, and professional routines rather than relying only on consumer chat adoption.

Source: https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2077047619260707263

## Anthropic commits C$10 million to Canada’s AI research ecosystem
Anthropic said it will invest C$10 million and partner with leading AI institutions in Canada to support new research. The move reinforces how major labs are building influence through academic ecosystem funding, talent pipelines, and national research partnerships—not just through product launches or API features.

Source: https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2077026346375540870

## Nokia unveils a commercial AI-native AI-RAN platform with NVIDIA
Nokia says it is launching the industry’s first commercial AI-native AI-RAN platform built on NVIDIA accelerated computing. For telecom researchers and operators, this is one of the clearest signs that AI-RAN is moving from architecture slides and consortium talk into actual commercial network stacks and vendor roadmaps.

Source: https://x.com/nokia/status/2077290224510435601

## Starlink introduces its next-generation V5 home internet kit
Starlink says its V5 kit has a smaller form factor, lighter design, better power efficiency, and speeds above 375 Mbps in supported areas. That is strategically important because LEO broadband competition now depends not just on satellite launch tempo, but also on terminal cost, energy draw, ease of installation, and consumer usability.

Source: https://x.com/Starlink/status/2077145785481957839

## OpenAI says Codex and ChatGPT Work have reached 7 million active users
OpenAI product lead Tibo Sottiaux said Codex and ChatGPT Work now have 7 million active users, and that users received a banked reset to replenish weekly limits. The signal here is adoption: AI coding and work-assistant products are clearly scaling into a major software category rather than staying confined to early adopters.

Source: https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2076735790567338203

## Hugging Face spotlights a 27B multimodal model that runs on a phone
Hugging Face amplified Bonsai 27B as the first 27B-class multimodal model reportedly able to run on a phone. If the deployment proves robust outside demo conditions, it would be another meaningful step toward serious on-device multimodal AI rather than small, heavily compromised mobile variants.

Source: https://x.com/huggingface/status/2077164993989017668

## Ericsson argues operators are nearing an autonomous-network inflection point
Ericsson, pointing to new Omdia-backed research, says 5G, cloud, and AI complexity are pushing communications service providers toward autonomous-network operations. For wireless systems work, this is an important operational trend: network automation is increasingly framed as a necessity for managing mixed, cloudified, AI-augmented infrastructure.

Source: https://x.com/ericsson/status/2077317388811354271

## Research Radar

### Fuse-then-Detect for Passive UAV Localization Using Multi-UE 5G Uplink Signals
**Authors:** Wenyu Huang, Nuria González-Prelcic, Vishnu Ratnam, Murat Bayraktar, Charlie Jianzhong Zhang  
**Venue:** arXiv

This paper is a strong pick for integrated sensing and communications. It uses uplink sounding reference signals from multiple user devices to localize UAVs without dedicated radar hardware, and reports a 4.84 m median 3D localization error in a cluttered urban FR1 setup.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11955

### HAPS-Complemented Terrestrial Networks
**Authors:** Animesh Yadav, Halim Yanikomeroglu  
**Venue:** arXiv

A relevant non-terrestrial networking paper that studies a full-duplex high-altitude platform station relaying traffic to cell-edge users. It is especially interesting for future 6G coverage architectures where terrestrial and aerial layers cooperate to improve edge performance.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12063

### AFDM-FTN: A Spectrally Efficient Waveform for High-Mobility Communications
**Authors:** Xianle Dai, Qu Luo, Jianguo Li, Fabien Heliot, Shuangyang Li, Lixia Xiao, Pei Xiao  
**Venue:** arXiv

This work proposes an AFDM-aided faster-than-Nyquist waveform aimed at high-mobility links, with accompanying low-complexity estimation and detection methods. It is a useful waveform-level addition to the broader push for spectrally efficient, mobility-robust future wireless systems.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12510
