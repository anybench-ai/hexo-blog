## Morning Take

This morning’s strongest pattern is operationalization. The most interesting moves are not just bigger models or louder launch announcements, but systems that are starting to change how science, agent tooling, workforce policy, and connectivity operate in practice.

## Top Stories

### OpenAI spotlighted GPT-5 Pro as a real scientific collaborator

OpenAI published a case study showing GPT-5 Pro helped immunologist Derya Unutmaz revisit a shelved T-cell experiment, generate a mechanistic hypothesis, and correctly predict an unpublished follow-up result. If this account holds up broadly, it is one of the clearest signs yet that frontier models can contribute meaningfully to scientific reasoning rather than just literature lookup or writing support.

Source: https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-immunology-mystery/

### Google DeepMind brought native computer use into Gemini 3.5 Flash

Google says computer use is now built directly into Gemini 3.5 Flash, enabling developers to build agents that can act across browser, mobile, and desktop interfaces. The important strategic shift is that tool use is being absorbed into the default model layer, which should make agent systems simpler to build and cheaper to maintain.

Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/

### RAISE US launched with Anthropic, Amazon, Microsoft, and the OpenAI Foundation as anchor partners

RAISE US launched as a bipartisan workforce initiative aimed at training, redeployment, and career transition in response to AI-driven labor disruption. What makes this notable is not just the coalition itself, but the fact that major AI actors are now participating in labor-transition infrastructure as a first-order policy issue.

Source: https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/news/raise-us-launches-uniting-nations-leading-employers-and-bipartisan-governors-behind-american-workers/

### Starlink is offering free service in earthquake-hit Venezuela through July 25

Starlink says it will provide free service to new and existing customers affected by the Venezuela earthquakes while it helps restore connectivity in the hardest-hit areas. This reinforces a broader pattern in LEO communications: satellite broadband is increasingly being treated as emergency-response infrastructure, not just a commercial internet product.

Source: https://x.com/Starlink/status/2070276549975372061

### Nokia joined Finland’s nationwide counter-drone border-security initiative

Nokia says its Defense unit will supply intelligent connectivity for the Finnish Border Guard’s next-generation counter-UAS effort, linking patrol vehicles, boats, sensors, and command systems in real time. This is a useful example of telecom-grade networking capabilities moving into national-security and sensing-heavy field operations.

Source: https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-to-provide-intelligent-connectivity-for-finnish-border-guard-counter-drone-initiative-nationwide/

### GitHub says its Copilot agentic harness is matching vendor agents with lower token burn

GitHub published benchmark results arguing that its shared Copilot harness reaches near-parity with model-vendor coding agents across multiple benchmarks while consuming fewer tokens in many runs. That matters because the next competitive layer in coding agents may be orchestration efficiency and tooling design, not just raw model capability.

Source: https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/evaluating-performance-and-efficiency-of-the-github-copilot-agentic-harness-across-models-and-tasks/

## Research Radar

### SpaceRipple: Lightweight Semantic Delivery for Mission-Oriented LEO Earth Observation Satellite Networks

**Authors:** Ziyi Yang, Hao Yuan, Yunxiang Yi, Wenbo Wang, Xing Zhang  
**Venue:** arXiv

SpaceRipple proposes sending mission-relevant semantic outputs instead of full raw imagery for Earth-observation satellite networks. That makes it especially interesting for bandwidth-constrained LEO systems where timely downlink decisions matter more than preserving every pixel.

🔗 http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26559v1

### Distributed Massive MIMO with 1-Bit Radio-over-Fiber Fronthaul: Uplink Spectral Efficiency and Power Control

**Authors:** Alireza Bordbar, Anzhong Hu, Giuseppe Durisi  
**Venue:** arXiv

This paper studies a distributed massive-MIMO design using 1-bit radio-over-fiber fronthaul and no local oscillators at access points. The appeal is practical: it points toward a lower-cost coherent fronthaul architecture while still analyzing spectral-efficiency and power-control tradeoffs carefully.

🔗 http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26939v1

### Multi-Modal Environment-Aware Beam Management for Massive MIMO: A Geometry-Driven Virtual Base Station Framework

**Authors:** Yijie Bian, Wei Guo, Jie Yang, Shenghui Song, Jun Zhang, Shi Jin  
**Venue:** arXiv

The authors use geometry and multimodal environmental information to improve beam management in high-frequency massive-MIMO settings. That is directly relevant to 6G-style environment-aware networking, where the control problem becomes too expensive if solved only through brute-force beam training.

🔗 http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26567v1
