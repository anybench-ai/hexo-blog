## AST SpaceMobile stages three more BlueBird satellites for launch

AST SpaceMobile says BlueBirds 14, 15, and 16 have arrived at Cape Canaveral. Back in Texas, spacecraft builds are underway through BlueBird 50, showing that the company is moving beyond one-off deployments toward an assembly-line cadence for direct-to-phone cellular broadband and government connectivity.

Source: [AST SpaceMobile](https://x.com/AST_SpaceMobile/status/2108752632789549373)

## NVIDIA weighs a deeper bet—or takeover—of Reflection AI

NVIDIA is reportedly in early talks to deepen its investment in Reflection AI, provide the startup with additional computing power, or acquire it outright. Reflection develops open-weight models, making the talks strategically important for NVIDIA as the company works to keep its hardware and software stack central to both open and proprietary AI development.

Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/nvidia-talks-invest-further-reflection-ai-or-buy-it-ft-reports-2026-10-10/)

## Sophos says OpenAI Daybreak cuts threat-investigation time by 96%

Sophos reports that its Daybreak-based agentic workflow reduced investigation time by 96% and now automates 52% of managed detection and response cases. Human analysts remain responsible for oversight and escalation, but the system can assemble evidence and draft findings in minutes rather than hours—a practical example of AI agents augmenting high-stakes operations.

Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sophos/)

## Asana makes browser-agent testing 76× cheaper and 5× faster

Asana used Codex to generate and evaluate more than 1,000 prompt variants for its browser agent. The resulting configuration preserved test quality while cutting model cost by 76 times and increasing speed fivefold, illustrating how automated prompt and model selection can materially change the economics of agent deployment.

Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/asana-browser-agent/)

## China launches its 27th group of low-orbit internet satellites

A Long March-12 rocket launched from the Hainan commercial spacecraft site at 3:27 a.m. Beijing time and placed China’s 27th group of low-orbit internet satellites into the planned orbit. The mission continues a rapid launch cadence that is turning China’s LEO broadband plans into a large operational constellation.

Source: [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261010/3977323ad9454a618ab5f0fc06be9324/c.html)

## Ericsson says AI can lift existing 5G performance by 25–30%

Ericsson says operators can improve existing 5G network performance by roughly 25–30% through AI-driven software, often without buying additional hardware. Much of the radio equipment deployed in 2025 and 2026 already supports the relevant features, potentially giving operators a lower-capex path to more capacity and efficiency before 6G arrives.

Source: [The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/cons-products/electronics/ai-can-enhance-5g-network-performance-by-30-ericsson/amp_articleshow/134860148.cms)

## Washington’s liability-first AI strategy raises a new blame question

The Trump administration is emphasizing legal liability as a mechanism for AI safety instead of prescribing a dense new layer of technical rules. That approach leaves a consequential question unresolved: when an autonomous system causes damage, responsibility may be contested among the model developer, application builder, and organization that deployed it.

Source: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-10/trump-s-ai-liability-push-opens-blame-game-for-models-gone-rogue)

## Research Radar

### 6G as It Is Actually Being Built: Insights from Early 3GPP Standardization

Ngo Hoang Tu, Huy T. Nguyen, Mao V. Ngo, Tran Thien Thanh, Vo Nguyen Quoc Bao, and Tony Q. S. Quek synthesize early Release 20 work across 3GPP’s RAN, SA, and CT groups. Their reading suggests that AI is becoming a network primitive, non-terrestrial access is becoming substrate rather than overlay, and deployability and multivendor testability matter more than wholesale architectural reinvention. The paper has been accepted by IEEE Network.

Source: [arXiv:2610.05694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05694)

### 6G NeXt — Towards 6G Split Computing Network Applications: Use Cases and Architecture

Sergiy Melnyk and colleagues develop a communication-and-computing architecture for two demanding 6G use cases: holographic communication and cooperative drone collision avoidance. Both require tightly coordinated distributed compute, wireless quality-of-service control, and clients that interact across the network rather than with a single centralized service.

Source: [arXiv:2610.08352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08352)

### LEO Signals-of-Opportunity for Navigation in Urban Environments: A GLRT-Assisted Off-Grid SBL Approach

Maedeh Sotoodenia, Mohammad Hossein Kahaei, and Alireza Nezamalhosseini combine generalized likelihood-ratio detection with off-grid sparse Bayesian learning and an extended Kalman filter for LEO signals-of-opportunity navigation. In simulated urban multipath and non-Gaussian noise, the approach cuts delay RMSE by up to 77% and position RMSE by 88.5% versus GLRT-based estimation.

Source: [arXiv:2610.07871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07871)
