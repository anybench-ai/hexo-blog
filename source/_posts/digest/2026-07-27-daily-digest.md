This morning’s digest leans heavily toward institutionalization: AI labs are deepening ties with governments, researchers, and telecom vendors, while direct-to-cell and O-RAN work keep moving from slideware into operating systems.

## 1) OpenAI expands its policy footprint with OpenAI Presence
OpenAI says its new global-affairs program now supports partners in 12 countries and will open a Seoul office in the second half of 2026. The important signal is organizational: model labs are no longer treating policy as a Washington-only function, but as something that needs sustained in-country relationships, local research coordination, and regulatory engagement.

Source: https://openai.com/global-affairs/openai-presence/

## 2) OpenAI launches a National Science Foundation partnership
OpenAI says a two-year NSF partnership will bring ChatGPT and up to $10 million in API credits to roughly 10,000 researchers, educators, and students across 400 institutions. This matters because it lowers the barrier for AI-assisted scientific work outside a small number of well-funded labs.

Source: https://openai.com/global-affairs/introducing-openai-for-the-national-science-foundation/

## 3) Google DeepMind expands the DOE Genesis Mission
Google DeepMind says it is committing $40 million in AI tokens and Google Cloud credits to help U.S. Department of Energy researchers use Gemini and other models to accelerate scientific discovery. The broader pattern is that frontier-model companies increasingly want to be the default compute-and-model layer for national-scale science programs.

Source: https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2079925576077324552

## 4) Google rolls out Gemini 3.6 Flash
Google says Gemini 3.6 Flash improves coding quality and token efficiency versus 3.5 Flash, and is rolling out in the Gemini app plus developer tools. That suggests Google is treating the “fast model” tier as a continuously optimized product surface for agents, coding, and high-volume production tasks.

Source: https://blog.google/technology/developers/gemini-3-6-flash/

## 5) Starlink Mobile turns on emergency SMS support near the Madrid wildfires
Starlink says it is providing free satellite-to-mobile connectivity for MASORANGE and Movistar customers in wildfire-affected regions near Madrid, with compatible LTE phones automatically falling back to SMS service. This is one of the most concrete public examples of direct-to-cell working as disaster-resilience infrastructure rather than as a speculative roadmap item.

Source: https://x.com/Starlink/status/2081382502560850198

## 6) Nokia pushes AI-native RAN from concept toward product
Nokia says its AI-native RAN platform, built with NVIDIA, has already shown more than 20% spectral-efficiency gains and targets more than 100% by 2028, with pilots starting later this year and commercial availability planned for 2027. For wireless research, the interesting angle is not just AI in the control loop, but the attempt to turn spectral-efficiency improvements into a software subscription story.

Source: https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-defines-the-next-era-of-radio-with-the-industrys-first-ai-native-ran-platform/

## 7) O-RAN Alliance ships 59 new or updated technical documents
O-RAN says its latest release wave adds 59 updated specs and reports, including new work on NTN connected-mode mobility, transport-network inclusion, SMO exposure, conflict management, and AI/ML-related interfaces. These are the sorts of standards-layer changes that quietly shape what vendors and operators can build over the next few years.

Source: https://www.o-ran.org/blog/59-new-or-updated-o-ran-technical-documents-released-since-march-2026

## 8) Anthropic offers science credits for rare-disease research
Anthropic says it will provide grants of up to $50,000 in Claude usage credits to researchers working to accelerate cures for rare diseases. It is not the biggest announcement of the week, but it does show continued movement toward domain-targeted AI science programs.

Source: https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2079256626771665098

## Research Radar

### Microwave Linear Analog Computers (MiLACs) for Communications: Opportunities and Challenges
**Authors:** Matteo Nerini, Bruno Clerckx  
**Venue:** arXiv

This paper argues that RF-domain analog linear computing could reduce digital beamforming and matrix-inversion costs in future large-array systems. If the ideas scale, they could matter for power-efficient massive MIMO and other next-generation wireless architectures.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22509

### Autonomous CSI Prediction Framework for O-RAN-Enabled 5G mmWave Vehicular Networks
**Authors:** Abidemi Orimogunje, Vukan Ninkovic, Nemanja Petrovic, Evariste Twahirwa, Gaspard Gashema, Lidija Fodor, Dejan Vukobratovic  
**Venue:** arXiv / IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology (accepted)

The paper proposes a self-trainable CSI prediction framework for proactive beam switching in 5G mmWave vehicular networks, using O-RAN-aligned ML applications and federated-learning options. It is especially relevant for mobility-heavy scenarios where reactive beam updates are too slow.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21963

### Location-Aware NAS Timer Optimization in NTN-TN Integrated Networks
**Authors:** Cheng Liu, Peng Hu  
**Venue:** arXiv

This work studies UE-specific NAS timer tuning in integrated terrestrial and non-terrestrial 5G systems, accounting for LEO path delays, queue exposure, and reliability. It is a useful systems paper for anyone thinking seriously about NTN registration behavior rather than just PHY-layer performance.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21947
