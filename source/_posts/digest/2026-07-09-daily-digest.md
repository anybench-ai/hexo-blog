# Morning Digest — Thursday, July 9

This morning’s mix felt less like isolated model hype and more like a full-stack reset: interface, evaluation, infrastructure, and telecom operations are all moving at the same time.

## 1) OpenAI finished the paid rollout of GPT-Live
OpenAI says GPT-Live is now fully rolled out to ChatGPT Go, Plus, and Pro users, with free-user rollout still underway. The strategic signal is bigger than a feature launch: high-quality voice is becoming a first-class product surface instead of a novelty layer on top of chat.

Source: https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2075019750569378007

## 2) OpenAI says SWE-Bench Pro is no longer a trustworthy frontier coding benchmark
OpenAI says it audited SWE-Bench Pro and found roughly 30% of tasks were broken, then retracted its prior recommendation that the benchmark be used as a leading coding eval. That matters because the field is entering the phase where benchmark integrity itself becomes a bottleneck to measuring progress.

Source: https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2074972179385720836

## 3) NVIDIA and LangChain are pushing an open blueprint for enterprise agents
NVIDIA highlighted a Deep Agents reference architecture built with LangChain, with the pitch centered on letting enterprises own, govern, customize, and iteratively improve agent systems over time. The real signal here is operational: agent stacks are being packaged less like demos and more like durable internal platforms.

Source: https://x.com/nvidia/status/2074872091388903774

## 4) NVIDIA says xAI’s Grok 4.5 was trained on GB300 NVL72 systems
NVIDIA publicly connected Grok 4.5 to its GB300 NVL72 infrastructure, using the launch as evidence that frontier training is increasingly inseparable from vendor-specific system architecture. Even with the obvious marketing angle, the post is useful because it shows how tightly model launches and hardware branding are now coupled.

Source: https://x.com/nvidia/status/2074979063106843131

## 5) Ericsson is extending AI-native automation from the RAN into the core network
Ericsson says its Intelligent Automation Platform is expanding from RAN automation into core-network operations, with cApps positioned as reusable automation building blocks. For telecom operators, that points to a broader shift from localized AI tooling toward AI-native network operations across the stack.

Source: https://x.com/ericsson/status/2075143044429439268

## 6) Nokia is pitching trusted connectivity as the foundation for defense AI
Nokia says it is working with NestAI to support mission planning, command-and-control, and threat-detection workflows with resilient connectivity underneath. The interesting angle is that AI adoption in defense is being framed not only as a model problem, but as a network assurance problem.

Source: https://x.com/nokia/status/2075107722647707712

## Research Radar

### 5G Positioning Reference Signal impact assessment in Non-Terrestrial Networks communication service
**Authors:** Alejandro Gonzalez-Garrido, Ottavio M. Picchi, Francesco Menzione  
**Venue:** arXiv

This paper studies how NR positioning reference signals behave in NTN communication scenarios, which matters if future satellite-cellular systems are expected to support positioning and navigation alongside broadband access.

Source: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07466v1

### Small Language Model-based Control for BBR over Low Earth Orbit Satellite Internet
**Authors:** Rakshitha De Silva, Shiva Raj Pokhrel, Jonathan Kua  
**Venue:** arXiv

This one is especially interesting because it applies a small language-model control layer to BBR under LEO-induced path variability, bridging transport optimization and lightweight AI rather than assuming giant models everywhere.

Source: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07142v1

### ORAN-DEFEND: Subspace Detection and Sanitization of Backdoor DRL xApps in Open RAN
**Authors:** Md Raihan Uddin, Fatemeh Lotfi, Tolunay Seyfi, Fatemeh Afghah  
**Venue:** arXiv

Open RAN security is going to become more important as third-party intelligence enters the control loop, and this paper directly targets the backdoor risk in DRL xApps.

Source: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06647v1
