## Morning Digest

### OpenAI turned its Bio Bug Bounty into a standing private program
OpenAI said it is evolving its Bio Bug Bounty into an ongoing private program and doubling top rewards to $50,000. The notable signal here is not just the payout increase, but the fact that frontier-model bio red teaming is being turned into a more permanent operational layer rather than a one-off publicity exercise.

Source: [https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2075647722766614733](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2075647722766614733)

### GPT-5.6 is now the preferred model in Microsoft 365 Copilot
Sam Altman said GPT-5.6 has become the preferred model in Microsoft 365 Copilot. That matters because it suggests the model is moving quickly from model-launch discussion into a high-distribution enterprise surface where capability improvements can alter real work routines at scale.

Source: [https://x.com/sama/status/2075585386441789605](https://x.com/sama/status/2075585386441789605)

### NVIDIA is framing AI factories as grid-flex infrastructure
NVIDIA promoted Emerald AI’s Conductor platform, built on its Vera Rubin DSX AI Factory reference design, as a way to make AI factories more power-flexible. The deeper pattern is that AI infrastructure is no longer just a compute race; energy coordination and grid compatibility are becoming strategic differentiators.

Source: [https://x.com/nvidia/status/2075724298841387291](https://x.com/nvidia/status/2075724298841387291)

### Starlink deployed 1,600+ kits after the Venezuela earthquakes
Starlink said it deployed more than 1,600 kits to support rescue teams, medical personnel, and humanitarian coordination after the Venezuela earthquakes. For anyone tracking LEO systems, this is a meaningful real-world use case showing satellite connectivity becoming part of emergency-response infrastructure.

Source: [https://x.com/Starlink/status/2075689795872084276](https://x.com/Starlink/status/2075689795872084276)

### The U.S. eased export rules for AI chips and commercial satellites to the UAE
According to Reuters, the U.S. made it easier to export certain military items, AI chips, and commercial satellites to the UAE. This is bigger than a trade-policy footnote: it shows how AI hardware and space systems are now being handled as strategic tools inside geopolitical alignment.

Source: [https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-makes-it-easier-to-export-certain-military-items-ai-chips-and-commercial-satellites-to-the-uae-4786613](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-makes-it-easier-to-export-certain-military-items-ai-chips-and-commercial-satellites-to-the-uae-4786613)

### Meta is reportedly aiming to put its own AI chip into production by September
Reuters reports that Meta is preparing its in-house AI training chip for production as early as September and is targeting roughly 14 gigawatts of compute capacity next year. If that holds, Meta joins the broader hyperscaler pattern of trying to own more of the silicon stack instead of relying purely on external vendors.

Source: [https://www.aol.com/articles/exclusive-meta-put-ai-chip-112222000.html](https://www.aol.com/articles/exclusive-meta-put-ai-chip-112222000.html)

## Research Radar

### Deep Reinforcement Learning-Empowered Wireless Sensor Networking for 6G Closed-Loop Controls
**Authors:** Chengleyang Lei, Wei Feng, Yunfei Chen, Yongxu Zhu  
**Venue:** arXiv

A good fit for Dad’s lane: it looks at how DRL could make 6G closed-loop control more adaptive in wireless sensor networking environments where static rules may be too brittle.

Source: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08272v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08272v1)

### RadioDiff-v2: Generative Angular Radio Maps for Multi-Beam Selection and Localization
**Authors:** Xiucheng Wang, Junxi Huang, Nan Cheng  
**Venue:** arXiv

This one stands out because it connects generative radio-map modeling with multi-beam selection and localization, which is directly relevant to future high-directionality wireless systems.

Source: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08045v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08045v1)

### Optimization and Deep Learning based Resource Allocation for UAV-Aided Wireless Communication with Rotatable Antenna Array
**Authors:** Fengcheng Pei, Lin Xiang, Anja Klein, Robert Schober  
**Venue:** arXiv

A strong aerial-networking pick: it mixes optimization and learning for resource allocation in UAV-assisted wireless communication, which is exactly the kind of adaptive control problem likely to matter more in future heterogeneous networks.

Source: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08420v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08420v1)
